Privacy Policy

Effective October 31, 2026

This policy explains how Lumora: Light Puzzles ("Lumora", "the app"), developed by Victor Dovgaliuc ("we", "us"), handles information. The short version: the only things the app sends us are anonymous crash reports and anonymous usage data about the puzzles, and you can turn each off.

In short
  • We do not collect your name, contact details or any account.
  • The app has no accounts, advertising or tracking.
  • When something goes wrong, the app sends an anonymous crash report to PostHog. You can turn this off in the app's Settings.
  • The app also sends anonymous usage data to PostHog, such as which puzzles are started, solved or left and when hints are used, so we can make the puzzles better. It is never linked to you. You can turn this off in the app's Settings too.
  • Your game progress is saved only on your device.
  • Purchases and Game Center are handled entirely by Apple.

Information stored on your device

To remember your game, Lumora saves the following on your device only: levels completed and stars earned, your shard balance, unlocked chapters and cosmetics, settings (sound, haptics, accessibility, reminders), daily reward and quest progress, and the identifiers of purchases already credited (so a purchase is never applied twice). This data stays on your device: crash reports never include it, and usage data includes only the few values listed below (for example, how many levels you have finished). It is removed when you delete the app. Depending on your device settings, it may be included in your own iCloud or computer backups, which are governed by Apple.

Crash reports (PostHog)

To find and fix problems, the app sends a report to PostHog, an error tracking service, when it crashes or when something fails that the app recovers from (for example, a purchase that could not be completed). Reports are processed on PostHog's servers in the United States and contain:

  • what went wrong: the error type and message, where in the code it happened, and which part of the app was involved (for a purchase error, the identifier of the item);
  • technical details: the app version, device model, iOS version, screen size, language, time zone and whether the device is on Wi-Fi or a mobile network;
  • a random identifier created when the app is installed, so reports from the same install can be grouped.

Reports never include your name, email, Apple Account, Game Center nickname, advertising identifier, game progress or anything you type. PostHog receives your device's IP address when a report is sent, as any internet service does; we have turned off looking up a location from it. We do not link reports to you, use them for advertising or tracking, or sell them, and we keep them only as long as they help us fix problems. Turn reports off at any time with Settings › Privacy › Send crash reports in the app. See the PostHog Privacy Policy.

Usage data (PostHog)

To see which puzzles are too hard, where players stop and whether players come back, the app sends short anonymous messages about how the game is played to PostHog, the same service as the crash reports, processed in the United States. They contain:

  • that the app was installed or opened, how many days ago it was installed, how many levels you have finished, and which version of the puzzles and of the app you have;
  • for each puzzle you start: which one it is (for example "chapter 2, level 5", the Daily, or an event), how many days after installing the app you started it, and whether you solved it, skipped it or left it, with the taps, hints, stars and seconds it took;
  • rewards and choices: claiming a daily reward, quest or star chest and the shards it gave, unlocking a chapter (and whether with stars, shards or a purchase), the score at the end of an endless run, whether you turned reminders on and whether you opened one, that the app suggested the Daily puzzle after your first boss level and whether you played it, and that you opened the share sheet for a result;
  • your Daily streak: how many days long it is when you solve a Daily, and when a streak freeze covers a missed day or a streak ends;
  • that the app asked iOS to show the App Store rating prompt (iOS decides whether it appears, and your rating or review goes to Apple, not to us);
  • in versions that sell items: opening the shop, and whether a purchase went through, with the identifier of the item (never payment details);
  • the same technical details and random identifier as crash reports, so messages from the same install can be counted together without knowing who you are.

Usage data never includes your name, email, Apple Account, Game Center nickname, advertising identifier, contacts, location or anything you type, and the app does not record your screen or your taps. We have turned off PostHog's user profiles and location lookup. We do not link usage data to you, combine it with data from other companies, use it for advertising or tracking, or sell it, and we keep it only as long as it helps us improve the game. Turn it off at any time with Settings › Privacy › Share anonymous usage data in the app; the app stops collecting it straight away. Crash reports have their own switch.

New puzzles from our website

From time to time the app downloads new puzzle packs (for example for a holiday) from our website, victordov.github.io, which is hosted by GitHub. The app asks for the list of packs at most every few hours while you use it. These requests contain nothing about you or your game; like any web request, they reveal your device's IP address and the app's name and version to GitHub. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.

In-app purchases

Lumora 1.0 is free and sells nothing: there are no in-app purchases or subscriptions. If a later version offers them, they will be processed by Apple through the App Store, and we will never receive your name, Apple ID, payment card or billing details.

Game Center

Leaderboards use Apple's Game Center and are optional. If you are signed in to Game Center, your scores for the Daily Challenge (number of taps) and Endless mode (furthest puzzle reached) are sent to Apple and shown to other players together with your Game Center nickname, according to your Game Center settings. We do not receive your Game Center account information. You can sign out of Game Center at any time in your device's Settings.

Notifications

If you choose to turn on reminders, Lumora schedules local notifications on your device (for example, when a new Daily Challenge is ready). They are created on the device itself; no push server is involved and the reminders send nothing anywhere (usage data, if on, notes only whether you turned them on and whether you opened one). You can turn them off in the app's settings or in your device's Settings.

Sharing your results

When you tap Share after solving a level, the app creates an image of the board and your score and hands it to the iOS share sheet. Nothing is shared unless you choose where to send it.

Children

Lumora is suitable for all ages. Crash reports and usage data are anonymous and are not linked to any player, so the app does not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your region).

Your choices and rights

Crash reports and usage data are anonymous, so we cannot tell which of them came from you, and there is no profile for us to access, correct or export. You can stop sending either at any time in the app's Settings, and you can contact us with any request. To remove everything Lumora has stored on your device, delete the app. Game Center data can be managed through Apple.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, for example if a future version adds a feature that requires collecting data, we will update this page and its effective date before that version is released.
